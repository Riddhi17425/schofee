How Banks and NBFCs Can Partner with Educational Institutions for Fee Financing

  • By Schofee Team
  • 22 October 2026
  • 8 min read

Education is an essential household expense, but paying school fees can create financial pressure for many families. Annual tuition fees, admission charges and other educational expenses may become due together, making it difficult for parents to manage the entire amount upfront.

For educational institutions, delayed fee payments can affect cash-flow planning, increase collection-related administrative work and create uncertainty around expected fee receipts. At the same time, banks and Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) have an opportunity to address this payment challenge through structured education-fee financing solutions.

School Fee Financing Partnerships allow lending institutions to collaborate with schools, colleges, coaching institutes and other educational organisations to provide eligible parents with financing options for education expenses.

Digital platforms such as Schofee support this ecosystem by connecting educational institutions, parents and financing partners through a digital education-fee financing process.

What Are School Fee Financing Partnerships?

School fee financing partnerships are arrangements in which banks or NBFCs collaborate with educational institutions to finance eligible education-fee payments.

Instead of requiring every parent to pay the entire fee from their immediate savings, an eligible parent may apply for financing through the participating lender. If the application is approved, the financed amount can be disbursed according to the agreed arrangement, potentially directly to the educational institution.

The parent then repays the financing provider through instalments under the applicable repayment schedule.

The partnership typically involves three primary stakeholders:

  • Banks and NBFCs: Evaluate financing applications, determine eligibility, establish loan terms and manage the lending relationship.
  • Educational institutions: Participate in the agreed fee-financing arrangement and communicate available payment options to parents.
  • Parents: Apply for financing, complete the required documentation and repay the financed amount according to the agreed terms.

A digital financing platform may help facilitate the application journey and coordination between these stakeholders, depending on the services and integrations available.

For lending institutions, this model offers a way to explore education-related financing through partnerships with organisations that regularly collect tuition and other education fees.

Why Should Banks and NBFCs Explore Education-Fee Financing?

Education-fee financing can create opportunities for lending institutions to serve a specific financial need while building relationships with educational organisations.

1

Access to an Education-Focused Lending Segment

School fees represent a recurring financial responsibility for many households. Financing solutions designed around these expenses can help lenders address a defined customer requirement.
Banks and NBFCs can evaluate whether education-fee financing fits their existing lending strategy, target customer profile and risk appetite.
The opportunity depends on the institution's parent base, the lender's eligibility criteria and the suitability of its financing products.

2

Institutional Partnership Opportunities

Working with schools and educational institutes can create a structured channel through which lenders introduce eligible parents to financing options.
Instead of relying exclusively on individual customer acquisition, lenders can explore partnerships with institutions that have an established relationship with families.
However, schools do not automatically become a source of approved borrowers. Applications still require the lender's prescribed assessment and approval process.

3

Support for Parent Affordability

Large education payments can be difficult for some families to manage at once. Financing can provide eligible parents with an alternative payment arrangement by spreading repayment over an agreed period.
For lenders, this creates an opportunity to develop products around a clearly defined education-related requirement.
The financing cost, repayment tenure and other terms should be transparent and appropriate to the product being offered.

4

Potential for Repeat Customer Relationships

Education expenses can arise across multiple academic years. A positive customer experience may create opportunities for lenders to maintain longer-term relationships with eligible families.
Any repeat financing remains subject to the lender's policies, the customer's circumstances and the terms applicable at the time of a new application.

5

Digital Lending Opportunities

A digital application process can help reduce friction in collecting application information and documents.
Depending on the platform and lender systems, digital workflows may support application submission, document collection, status communication and coordination with participating institutions.
Lenders should evaluate the actual capabilities, integration requirements and operational responsibilities before selecting a technology partner.

How School Fee Financing Partnerships Work

A school fee financing partnership requires coordination between the lender, educational institution and parent.

The following is a typical process, although the exact steps depend on the lender and partnership agreement.

  1. Identify Suitable Educational Institutions

    Banks and NBFCs identify schools, colleges, coaching institutes or other educational organisations that fit their partnership strategy.
    Relevant evaluation factors may include the institution's fee structure, operating history, parent profile, payment processes and expected financing requirements.

  2. Define the Partnership Model

    The lender and institution determine how the fee-financing arrangement will operate.
    This includes establishing responsibilities, the applicable financing model, payment settlement process, customer communication and support procedures.
    Commercial terms should be documented before the programme is introduced to parents.

  3. Introduce Financing Options to Parents

    The participating institution communicates the available financing option to interested parents.
    Parents should receive clear information about eligibility, financing costs, repayment schedules, required documentation and the identity of the lending institution.
    Participation should not be presented as guaranteed approval.

  4. Complete the Digital Application

    Interested parents submit the required information and documents through the applicable application process.
    Depending on the lender's requirements, this may include identity verification, financial information, consent and other documentation.

  5. Assess Eligibility and Creditworthiness

    The bank or NBFC evaluates the application under its lending policies and applicable regulatory requirements.
    The lender determines whether to approve the application and establishes the relevant financing terms.

  6. Disburse the Approved Amount

    After approval and completion of the required formalities, the lender disburses the financed amount according to the agreement.
    Where direct institutional settlement is supported, the applicable amount may be paid to the educational institution under the agreed conditions.

  7. Manage Repayment and Customer Support

    The parent repays the financing provider according to the agreed schedule.
    The lender remains responsible for the applicable lending and repayment processes, while the institution and any technology partner perform their respective contractual responsibilities.
    Clearly defined processes help avoid confusion regarding fee settlement, repayment obligations and customer queries.

No-Cost EMI vs Low-Cost EMI: Partnership Models for Banks and NBFCs

An important consideration in school fee financing is deciding how the financing cost will be handled.

Schofee describes two partnership models: No-Cost EMI and Low-Cost EMI. The difference is primarily the party bearing the applicable financing or discounting cost.

1. No-Cost EMI Model

Under Schofee's described No-Cost EMI model, the educational institution bears the applicable financing or discounting cost under the agreed arrangement. This allows the parent to access the offered EMI structure without bearing that financing cost.

Potential benefits include:

  • An additional payment option for parents.
  • Potential support for parent affordability and admission-related payment requirements.
  • A financing arrangement that may help institutions manage approved fee payments.
  • An opportunity for lenders to participate in an institution-supported financing model.

What lenders should evaluate

Banks and NBFCs should assess the institution's agreed commercial arrangement, settlement process, documentation requirements and the financial structure of the programme.

The institution's willingness to bear financing costs should be established contractually. The model should not be assumed to have zero costs for every stakeholder.

2. Low-Cost EMI Model

Under Schofee's described Low-Cost EMI model, the parent bears the applicable financing cost, while the institution receives the agreed fee settlement without the institutional discounting cost specified under this model.

Potential benefits include:

  • Financing flexibility for eligible parents.
  • A partnership model that does not require the institution to bear the specified discounting cost.
  • An alternative structure for institutions evaluating financing-based fee collection.
  • An opportunity for lenders to offer education-related financing under defined repayment terms.

What lenders should evaluate

The lender should clearly communicate the applicable financing cost, instalment amount, repayment period and other charges.

Parents should be able to understand the total repayment obligation before accepting the financing offer.

Benefits of School Fee Financing Partnerships for Banks and NBFCs

1

A Defined Education-Financing Use Case

School fee financing addresses a specific expense rather than an unrestricted borrowing requirement.
This gives lenders a defined use case around which they can evaluate product design, eligible customer segments and repayment structures.
The actual credit risk and suitability of each application must still be assessed individually.

2

Institutional Distribution Opportunities

Partnerships may help lenders reach parents through participating educational institutions.
A well-defined process can clarify how financing options are communicated and how interested parents enter the application journey.
The quality of the opportunity depends on the institution's parent base, customer participation, lender eligibility requirements and conversion rates.

3

Potential Operational Efficiencies

Digital application and documentation workflows may reduce certain manual steps involved in collecting information and coordinating applications.
However, the actual operational benefit depends on the technology platform, integration depth, internal lender processes and the allocation of responsibilities between partners.

4

Opportunities for Product Development

Banks and NBFCs can evaluate whether education-fee financing requires specific application journeys, repayment structures or institution-level partnership arrangements.
Product development should be based on customer needs, affordability assessment, regulatory requirements and measurable programme performance.

5

A Broader Education-Financing Strategy

School fee financing can be evaluated as part of a lender's wider education-related lending strategy.
Institutions may have different fee structures and customer requirements, so lenders should assess each proposed partnership individually rather than assume that a single model will suit every organisation.

How Educational Institutions Benefit from Financing Partnerships

A successful financing partnership should also create practical value for the participating educational institution.

Potential benefits include:

  • Improved settlement predictability: Approved financed payments may be settled according to agreed timelines.
  • Parent payment flexibility: Eligible families can access additional payment options.
  • Reduced collection follow-ups: Financing may reduce collection-related work for approved fees covered by the arrangement.
  • Better financial planning: Defined settlement processes can help institutions plan around expected receipts.
  • Structured payment administration: Clear responsibilities can support coordination between the institution, lender and technology partner.

These benefits are not automatic. Schools should evaluate the settlement terms, applicable costs, operational requirements and parent experience before adopting a programme.

Key Considerations Before Launching a Financing Partnership

Banks and NBFCs should establish clear operational, financial and compliance requirements before launching a school fee financing programme.

  1. Credit Risk and Eligibility

    Define the eligible borrower profile, required documentation, underwriting criteria and approval process.
    The lender should retain responsibility for its credit decisions and ensure that financing is offered according to its applicable policies.

  2. Financing Costs and Commercial Terms

    Document the financing or discounting cost, settlement amounts, payment timelines and responsibilities of each party.
    For No-Cost EMI and Low-Cost EMI models, all relevant commercial arrangements should be clearly defined.

  3. Regulatory Compliance and Customer Disclosures

    Ensure that the financing product and partnership comply with applicable lending, consumer protection, privacy and data-handling requirements.
    Parents should receive clear disclosures about the lender, financing cost, repayment obligations and applicable contractual terms.
    Where required, relevant loan documentation and Key Facts Statements should be provided in accordance with applicable requirements.

  4. Data Security and Consent

    Define how parent information and documents will be collected, accessed, transferred and retained.
    Appropriate consent, access controls, security measures and contractual responsibilities should be established for all participating parties.

  5. Disbursement and Reconciliation

    Confirm how approved financing amounts will be disbursed, how settlement will be verified and how discrepancies will be resolved.
    Institutions should understand which payments are covered by the financing arrangement and how non-financed balances will be managed.

  6. Customer Support and Grievance Handling

    Establish clear procedures for application queries, rejected applications, repayment concerns, cancellations and complaints.
    Parents should know which organisation is responsible for each part of the process.

  7. Programme Performance

    Track relevant indicators such as application volumes, approval rates, disbursement values, settlement timelines, repayment performance and customer complaints.
    These measures can help lenders and institutions assess the effectiveness of the programme and identify areas for improvement.

Frequently asked questions

What Are School Fee Financing Partnerships?

School fee financing partnerships are arrangements in which banks or NBFCs collaborate with educational institutions to finance eligible fee payments. Approved amounts are disbursed according to the agreement, and parents repay the lender under the applicable terms.

Why Should Banks and NBFCs Partner With Educational Institutions?

These partnerships can create an opportunity to offer education-related financing through institutional relationships, address parent payment needs and evaluate a defined lending use case.
The benefits depend on borrower eligibility, institutional participation, programme costs and lending performance.

What Is the Difference Between No-Cost EMI and Low-Cost EMI?

Under Schofee's described models, the institution bears the applicable financing or discounting cost under No-Cost EMI, while the parent bears the applicable financing cost under Low-Cost EMI.
The actual terms depend on the agreement and financing offer.

Who Approves the Financing Application?

The participating bank or NBFC evaluates the application according to its eligibility criteria, underwriting policies and applicable requirements. Applying through an educational institution or digital platform does not guarantee approval.

How Is the School Fee Disbursed?

After approval and completion of the required formalities, the financed amount is disbursed according to the agreed arrangement. Direct payment to the institution depends on the applicable partnership and settlement terms.

Can a Digital Platform Replace the Lender's Underwriting Process?

Not automatically. A digital platform may facilitate application and documentation workflows, but the lender remains responsible for the credit assessment and decisions assigned to it under the applicable arrangement.

How Can Banks and NBFCs Evaluate a School Fee Financing Partnership?

They should assess the institution's requirements, target borrower profile, financing model, credit risk, commercial terms, regulatory obligations, settlement process and customer support arrangements.
